--------------------------------------------------------------
[0:20] INTRO — What is this game?
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: walking around the Hideout -> entering the portal -> the map rising up]

Hey everyone, this is the first devlog for the game I'm making on my own.
It doesn't have a name yet. On my machine it's just called Project-A. (laughs)

It's an ARPG in the style of Path of Exile, with an angled top-down camera.
You've got a home base called the Hideout, you step through a portal, and you land in a map that's randomly generated every single time.
Go in, kill monsters, get loot, head home, repeat.

[VISUAL: zoom in on the pencil lines on the character and trees]

And I want the whole thing to look like it was drawn in pencil. More on that later.

[VISUAL: CLAUDE.md page or a host / client diagram]

One more thing I decided on day one: I want you to be able to play with friends, parties of two to six.
Right now it's still single player, but every bit of code is written with that in mind.
That's going to come up a lot in this video.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[1:15] Day 1 — Ground that builds itself
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: hitting regenerate over and over, the map changing every time]

I started with the map generator, because that's the heart of this genre.
At first it was a grassy island with a random shape, scattered trees and rocks, and an underside hanging below.

[VISUAL: the map build animation, ground rising piece by piece]

Then I made the ground rise up like this when you enter a map.
Totally unnecessary. But I love it. (smiles)

[VISUAL: capsule character walking with the camera following]

Same day, I got the isometric camera working, plus a character that was still just a capsule walking around.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[2:00] Terraced maps + seeds
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: multi-level map with ramps, cliffs and ponds]

Flat maps get boring fast, so I added multiple levels, ramps, cliffs and ponds.
Some ponds are shallow enough to wade through.

[VISUAL: same seed twice, identical maps side by side]

The important part: everything is generated from a single number, the "seed".
Same seed, exact same map, every time.

Why does that matter? Because when you play with friends, I don't have to send the whole map to every machine.
I just send one number, and every player builds the same map locally. That saves a ton of bandwidth.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[2:45] Movement that feels good
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: walking up ramps, sliding along walls, multi-jumping, tap dash, hold sprint]

I sank a lot of time into making movement feel good.
No getting stuck on ramps, sliding along walls when you bump into them, multiple jumps,
and one button that dashes when you tap it and sprints when you hold it.

[VISUAL: walking behind a tree/cliff, the occluder fading with an outline]

This camera angle has a classic problem: you walk behind a tree and you can't see yourself.
So anything that blocks the view now fades out and shows an outline.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[3:30] Hideout and portals
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: Hideout -> walk to the portal with the "Click me!!" sign -> click -> fade to black -> map]

Every game like this needs a home base, so I split things into two scenes: the Hideout and the random map.
Walk up to the portal, click it, the screen fades out, the scene loads, and it fades back in.

[VISUAL: going back and forth through portals, same character the whole time]

It's the same character the whole way through, it's not recreated every time,
so whatever you're carrying and all your stats don't vanish when you warp.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[4:10] Day 5 — The character comes alive + pencil style
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: capsule swapped for a human model walking, running, jumping, landing]

The capsule is now a person, with walk, run and jump animations.
I split the jump into four parts: crouch, rising, falling and landing.

(slightly ranty)
There was a funny bug too: running up a ramp made the character play the jump animation.
I was guessing "is he jumping?" from vertical speed, and going up a ramp... has vertical speed.
The fix was to check whether you actually pressed jump.

[VISUAL: dash leaving ghost copies behind (slow-mo)]

And dashing leaves Rockman-style afterimages. I'm really happy with this one.

[VISUAL: before/after pencil shader — split-screen wipe]

Now, the art style.
I wrote a shader that finds the edges of everything in the scene and draws them as pencil lines.
The lines wobble slightly like they're hand-drawn, they're doubled up, and they have a graphite texture.
Put that on the character, the trees and the grass, and suddenly the game has a personality.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[5:15] Days 6–7 — Punching works!
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: first punch on an enemy, damage numbers, enemy flying back and dying]

This is where it starts feeling like a game.
You can punch, there are damage numbers, and enemies fly back in the direction you hit them when they die.

[VISUAL: nav graph gizmo showing AI paths across the map]

Enemies can navigate a random map because every time a map is generated, I also build a navigation graph for the AI:
where you can walk, where you need to jump up, where you can jump down.

[VISUAL: a pack wandering -> one spots you -> the whole pack charges / an enemy jumping down a cliff]

Monsters come in packs and wander around together. When one spots you, the whole pack comes running.
And they can jump between levels to chase you.

[VISUAL: a big glowing Elite]

There are Elites too: bigger, glowing, more health, hit harder.

(a bit more serious)
Quick note on the code here.
When you attack, your side of the game doesn't say "I dealt 50 damage", it says "I'd like to punch here."
Then the server side decides whether you can, who you hit and for how much.
That way, once it's online, cheaters can't just edit their own numbers.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[6:30] The gem skill system
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: PlayerSkills Inspector showing slots + gems]

Skills work like in PoE: they come from gems.
An active gem is the move itself, and you can socket up to five support gems to modify it,
like faster attacks, bigger area, more damage.

[VISUAL: Punch combo Boxing / Hook / Uppercut while walking — legs walking, arms punching]

The first one is a punch. It picks random moves without repeating, and it only uses the upper body, so your legs keep walking. No need to stop.

[VISUAL: Hurricane Kick]

Hurricane Kick.

[VISUAL: holding Flicker Strike, chaining through a whole pack (best slow-mo shot)]

And my favourite: Flicker Strike.
You teleport next to the enemy closest to your cursor and hit it. Hold the button and you chain through the whole pack.

[VISUAL: shadow clones punching together]

Plus shadow clones that come out and punch alongside you.
And they're not just for show, the clones deal real damage.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[7:30] Day 9 — Items and loot
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: Project window Items folder -> item Inspector]

Once you can fight, you need loot.
Right now there are two kinds of items: gear, like helmets, boots and rings, and consumables, like potions.
Item stats use the exact same formula as gems. I wrote that formula once and the whole game uses it.

[VISUAL: killing an enemy, nothing on the ground -> warp home -> click the chest -> loot list pops up]

Here's where I went a different way from PoE: loot doesn't drop on the ground.
When an enemy dies, the loot goes straight into a chest back home. Clear the map, head home, and see what you got.
Icons on the left, and the name colour tells you the rarity.

[VISUAL: loot in the chest -> don't open it -> enter a new map -> come back, chest empty]

But if you forget to check it and jump straight into a new map... all your old loot is gone.
(pause) That's on purpose. I want going home and opening the chest to be part of the rhythm of the game.
If you think that's too harsh, come fight me in the comments. (laughs)

[VISUAL: zoom on the icons]

The icons are just simple shapes drawn by code for now. Real art will come later.


--------------------------------------------------------------
[8:40] Work in progress
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: walking behind a cliff with a circular hole cut around the character (if you can record it)]

Right now I'm working on a new way to handle things blocking your view.
Instead of fading the whole object, it cuts a circular hole around you, with a soft edge, and the shadows stay intact.
I'm also fixing Flicker Strike sometimes teleporting you under the ground when the enemy is standing next to a cliff. (laughs)


--------------------------------------------------------------
[9:05] Recap + next time
--------------------------------------------------------------
[VISUAL: quick montage from bare grass -> map rising -> capsule -> human -> pencil -> combat -> Flicker -> loot chest]

Nine days, twenty-eight commits.
From one patch of grass to random maps, a character, skills, monsters and loot.

But there's still a lot missing.
No inventory yet, so you can't actually take items out of the chest and wear them.
No potion hotkeys, no health bar on screen.
And you can't play with friends yet.

Next episode will probably be the inventory and an equipment screen.
And if things go well, maybe a first try at two-player co-op.
